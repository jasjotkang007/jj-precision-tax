TAX RESOURCES · BENEFITS

Why You Must Update CRA About Life Changes

Benefits may depend on marital status, family net income, province or territory, eligible children and other facts. When those details change, benefit entitlement can change too.

Marital status changes

Marriage, becoming common-law, separation or divorce can change family income calculations and benefit eligibility. CRA generally asks you to report a marital-status change by the end of the month following the month it occurs. Special rules apply to separation.

Moving provinces or territories

Provincial and territorial programs differ. Moving can affect eligibility for credits and benefits. Keep your address current and use the correct province of residence information for your tax return.

How overpayments happen

If CRA continues paying using outdated information, you may receive more than you are entitled to. CRA may later recalculate past payments and ask you to repay an overpayment.

Income and annual returns

Many benefits are recalculated using tax-return information. Both partners may need to file. Also respond to CRA requests about address, custody, banking or other eligibility details.

Practical checklist

Update your address and marital status through the appropriate CRA channel, review custody and dependant details, file your annual return and keep confirmation of reported changes.

Example

A couple separates but benefits continue to be calculated using former family circumstances. After CRA processes the change, entitlement may be recalculated and an overpayment may arise.
