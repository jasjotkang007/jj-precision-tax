TAX RESOURCES · MOVING

Can I Claim Moving Expenses on My Canadian Tax Return?

Moving to another city does not automatically make moving costs tax-deductible. CRA generally allows eligible moving expenses for qualifying work, business or full-time study moves when distance and other conditions are met.

The 40-kilometre test

Your new home generally must be at least 40 kilometres closer to the new work location or school, measured by the shortest usual public route. Other conditions must also be met.

Who may qualify?

Employees and self-employed people moving to work or operate a business at a new location may qualify. Eligible full-time post-secondary students may qualify under narrower rules, generally against certain taxable scholarship, bursary, fellowship, research grant or prize income.

Which costs may qualify?

Depending on the facts, eligible expenses may include movers or truck rental, travel for the move, certain temporary lodging and meals, costs to sell an old home and some costs for a vacant former home while trying to sell it. Limits and records requirements apply.

What generally cannot be claimed?

Personal house-hunting trips, job-hunting travel and losses on a home sale are generally not deductible. Employer reimbursements affect the amount you may claim.

How is the deduction used?

For a work-related move, eligible expenses are generally deducted from eligible employment or self-employment income earned at the new work location. They generally cannot reduce unrelated investment income.

Records and form

Keep receipts, travel details, invoices and information about the new work location or school. Form T1-M is used to calculate moving expenses.

Example

Someone moves for a new job and the new home is at least 40 kilometres closer to that work location. If other conditions are met, some eligible moving costs may be deductible. Moving solely for lifestyle reasons may not qualify.

Note: Illustrative only; eligibility and results depend on the relevant tax-year rules and individual facts.
