Which Employment Expenses Can Employees Deduct?

Employees cannot automatically deduct every work-related cost. Eligibility depends on the job, expenses paid, employer requirements, reimbursements and CRA rules for each expense.

Start with Form T2200

For many employment expenses, your employer must complete Form T2200, Declaration of Conditions of Employment, confirming that your employment conditions required you to pay the expenses. Keep the completed form; do not normally attach it to your return. You still need to meet the rules for each expense. If you have more than one employer, ask each relevant employer for a separate T2200.

Working from home

For 2023 and later tax years, employees claiming eligible work-space-in-the-home expenses use Form T777, Statement of Employment Expenses, and generally need a completed Form T2200 from their employer. Form T2200 is kept with your records; Form T777 is filed with your return. The temporary flat-rate method applied only to 2020–2022. Eligible costs may include a work-related share of rent, utilities and maintenance, subject to the detailed rules. Employees cannot deduct mortgage principal or claim expenses for days they did not meet the eligibility conditions.

Travel to other work locations

Home-to-regular-office commuting is usually personal. Different rules may apply when an employee must travel to client sites or other locations as part of the job. Keep a vehicle log, receipts and details of allowances or reimbursements.

Meals and lodging

For ordinary employees, eligible travel expenses may include transportation, meals and lodging when the employment conditions require travel and the employee pays the costs. Form T2200 is generally required, and allowable expenses are calculated on Form T777 and claimed on line 22900. For food and beverages on qualifying work travel, the deduction is generally limited to 50% of the lesser of the actual cost and a reasonable amount. The employee generally must be required to be away for at least 12 consecutive hours from the municipality/metropolitan area of the employer’s location where they normally report. Subtract reimbursements and do not claim costs paid by the employer.

Transportation employees and truck drivers

Transportation employees may have separate rules. Employees claiming eligible meals and lodging using the special transportation-employee process need Form TL2, Claim for Meals and Lodging Expenses: the employee completes Parts 1 and 2 and the employer completes Part 3. The employer must also complete Form T2200. Eligible long-haul truck drivers may use the special deductible percentage for qualifying trips; eligibility depends on the driver’s duties, qualifying trips and hours. Keep trip logs, dates, receipts and employer records. Form TL2 is retained with records and is not normally attached to the return.

What is generally not deductible?

Ordinary commuting, personal clothing, personal meals and reimbursed expenses are generally not deductible as employment expenses, subject to specific exceptions.

Example

Driving from home to a regular office is usually personal commuting. Driving from the office to customer locations during the workday may qualify for business-use vehicle expenses if conditions and records are met.

Note: Illustrative only; eligibility and results depend on the relevant tax-year rules and individual facts.

Keep good records

Keep the completed T2200, receipts, mileage logs, workspace calculations, travel dates and purposes, allowance/reimbursement records, and TL2 where applicable. File Form T777 with your return when claiming these expenses; keep T2200 and TL2 with your records in case CRA asks for them.
