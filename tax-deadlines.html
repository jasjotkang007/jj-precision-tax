IMPORTANT DATES

Tax Deadlines.
Plan ahead.

Use this as a starting point for common Canadian tax dates. Your actual deadline can differ based on your business structure, fiscal year-end, filing method, special circumstances or a weekend/holiday rule.

Reviewed: October 2026 · Always confirm the applicable year with CRA.

Common Filing & Payment Deadlines

Most Individuals

Filing: Generally April 30 following the tax year.

Balance owing: Generally due April 30, even if a filing extension applies.

CRA individual dates ↗

Self-Employed Individuals

Filing: Generally June 15 for the individual and spouse/common-law partner where the self-employed filing rule applies.

Balance owing: Generally still due April 30.

CRA individual dates ↗

Corporations

T2 return: Generally due six months after the corporation's tax year-end.

Corporate tax balance: The payment due date is generally earlier than the return filing deadline and depends on the corporation's circumstances.

CRA corporation return guidance ↗

GST/HST

Filing and payment due dates depend on the reporting period and filing frequency. Monthly, quarterly and annual filers can have different due dates; annual sole-proprietor rules can also vary.

CRA GST/HST filing dates ↗

Payroll Remittances

Remittance frequency and due dates depend on the employer's remitter type. Missing a payroll remittance can result in penalties and interest.

CRA payroll remittances ↗

RRSP Contributions

The contribution deadline for a deduction for a particular tax year is announced annually by CRA. Confirm the exact date each year before relying on it. FHSA contributions follow different timing rules.

CRA RRSP guidance ↗
Do not rely on this page as a personalized deadline calendar. If a deadline is close, confirm it directly with CRA and consider processing time for payments or electronic submissions. Keep proof of filing and payment.
