IMPORTANT DATES

Tax Deadlines.
Plan ahead.

These are the key dates and timing rules to keep on your calendar for the current tax year. Personal filing dates fall in the following calendar year. Business deadlines depend on your reporting period, fiscal year-end and CRA-assigned remitter type; use the CRA account or notice for your exact due date.

Reviewed: October 2026 · Always confirm the applicable year with CRA.

Common Filing & Payment Deadlines

Personal Income Tax

Return filing: April 30 of the following calendar year for most individuals.

Balance owing: April 30 of the following calendar year, even if you file later.

Quarterly instalments in 2026: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

CRA individual dates ↗

Self-Employed Individuals

Return filing: June 15 of the following calendar year if you or your spouse/common-law partner carried on a business. The balance owing is still due April 30.

Quarterly instalments: March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15, if required.

CRA individual dates ↗

Corporate Income Tax

T2 return: Due within 6 months after the corporation’s tax year-end. Example: a December 31 year-end means the T2 is due June 30.

Balance owing: Usually 2 months after year-end. A qualifying Canadian-controlled private corporation may have 3 months if it meets CRA conditions.

Instalments: Usually monthly; eligible small corporations may qualify for quarterly instalments. Confirm the corporation’s schedule.

CRA corporation return guidance ↗

GST/HST Returns

Monthly or quarterly filers: Return and payment are due 1 month after the reporting period ends. Example: a quarter ending March 31 is due April 30.

Most annual filers: Due 3 months after fiscal year-end. A sole proprietor with a December 31 year-end and business income generally pays by April 30 and files by June 15.

Instalments: If required, due within 1 month after each fiscal quarter.

CRA GST/HST filing dates ↗

Payroll Source Deductions

Regular remitter: CRA must receive deductions by the 15th of the following month.

Quarterly remitter: April 15, July 15, October 15 and January 15.

Accelerated threshold 1 ($25,000–$99,999.99 average monthly withholding amount): deductions for paydays on the 1st–15th are due by the 25th of that month; paydays on the 16th–month-end are due by the 10th of the next month.

Accelerated threshold 2 ($100,000+): due within 3 working days after each 7-day remitting period. Check your CRA-assigned schedule.

CRA payroll remittances ↗

RRSP Contributions

RRSP contributions: contributions made during the tax year and in the first 60 days of the following calendar year may be deductible, subject to your available RRSP deduction limit. The exact deadline can shift because of weekends or holidays, so confirm the date with CRA.

FHSA: the RRSP first-60-days rule does not apply. Contributions generally count in the calendar year they are made; unused FHSA participation room can carry forward under the applicable rules.

CRA RRSP guidance ↗
Calendar rule: If a due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or CRA-recognized public holiday, CRA generally treats it as on time if received on the next business day. Payroll rules are especially sensitive to the remitter type. Do not rely on this page as a personalized deadline calendar. If a deadline is close, confirm it directly with CRA and consider processing time for payments or electronic submissions. Keep proof of filing and payment.
