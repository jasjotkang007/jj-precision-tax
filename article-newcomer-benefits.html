Is There a “Welcome to Canada” Payment?

There is no general federal benefit officially called a “Welcome to Canada” payment. What people describe may be a combination of eligible family benefits, provincial or territorial benefits, and amounts paid for earlier eligible periods after an application is processed.

What payments might be involved?

There is no general federal benefit officially called a “Welcome to Canada” payment. People may be referring to the Canada Child Benefit (CCB), the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit (CGEB, formerly the GST/HST credit) or provincial and territorial benefits. Each program has its own eligibility rules and application process.

Why might the first payment be a lump sum?

After an application is processed, CRA may determine that the person was eligible for earlier months. Where the program rules permit retroactive payments, the first payment can include amounts for more than one month or quarter. It is not necessarily a bonus for arriving in Canada: it may be a catch-up payment for an earlier eligible period.

A more realistic timeline example

Suppose a parent and child arrive in Canada in March and the parent establishes Canadian tax residency in March. The parent applies for the relevant benefits in July, and CRA completes processing in September. If the family meets the program's eligibility rules from an earlier month, CRA may calculate benefits for eligible months before September rather than paying only from the processing month. The actual start month depends on the specific benefit and the family's facts; arrival date alone does not guarantee eligibility.

The amount may also depend on the family’s adjusted family net income, marital status, number and ages of children, residency and immigration-status requirements, and information CRA requests. For newcomers, CRA may ask for world-income information for the relevant period before becoming a Canadian tax resident, as well as income information after residency and tax-return details. These facts can affect eligibility and the amount paid. Do not assume CRA uses only the Canadian income earned after arrival.

Illustration only: A September payment might include eligible amounts calculated for several earlier months. It is not possible to determine the payment amount from arrival month alone, and different benefits can have different start dates and payment schedules.

What are RC66 and RC151?

RC66 is the Canada Child Benefits Application. RC151 is the GST/HST Credit Application for Individuals Who Become Residents of Canada. The forms and supporting information required depend on your circumstances and the benefits you are applying for. CRA may request documents to verify residency, status, family details or income.

Do I need to file if I had no Canadian income?

Often, filing is still important. Benefit amounts can depend on family net income and tax-return information even when no income tax is payable. A spouse or common-law partner may also need to file. Follow CRA instructions about reporting world income and income earned before and after becoming a resident.

What should I do?

Identify the specific benefit, check its eligibility rules, file required tax returns, submit the appropriate application and respond promptly to CRA requests. If a payment seems larger than expected, review the notice or statement showing the period covered before assuming it is an additional one-time benefit.
