Why Don't I Get My Tuition Fees Back as a Tax Refund?

Eligible tuition fees generally create a non-refundable tax credit. It can reduce income tax you owe, but generally cannot create a cash refund when you have no tax payable.

What does non-refundable mean?

An eligible tuition amount generally gives rise to a non-refundable tax credit. The credit can reduce income tax payable, but it does not refund the tuition bill itself and generally cannot create a refund when tax payable is already zero. For the federal tuition tax credit, the rate is 15%: $1,000 of eligible tuition amounts represents a potential federal credit of $150. The credit is non-refundable, so it can reduce federal tax payable but cannot by itself create a refund beyond tax paid or refundable credits. Provincial or territorial rules are separate.

Example: no tax payable

Suppose a student has $8,000 of eligible tuition fees. At the 15% federal tuition tax credit rate, that amount represents a potential federal credit of $1,200 ($8,000 × 15%). It is a tax credit, not a $1,200 tuition refund. If the student has no federal income tax payable to reduce, the unused eligible amount can generally be carried forward, subject to the rules. Provincial or territorial treatment may differ; Ontario no longer offers a provincial tuition tax credit for current tuition amounts.

Example: tuition increases a tax refund

Tuition can still make a difference when someone has income tax deducted from their pay. Suppose an employee has $35,000 of income, $3,000 of income tax deducted by their employer, and a tax calculation that shows $2,000 of income tax payable before applying an available tuition credit. Without that credit, the estimated refund would be $1,000 ($3,000 deducted minus $2,000 payable).

For example, $4,000 of eligible federal tuition amounts would represent a potential federal credit of $600 ($4,000 × 15%). If the person has at least $600 of federal tax payable available to reduce, and the full credit can be used, tax payable could fall from $2,000 to $1,400. With $3,000 already deducted at source, the estimated refund would rise from $1,000 to $1,600. The extra $600 is the tax reduction—not a reimbursement of tuition fees. Actual results depend on all credits, tax payable and provincial or territorial rules.

Illustration only: The $600 reduction is an assumed result for demonstrating the concept, not a promise of a particular credit. Actual savings depend on the tax year, eligible balance, federal and provincial rules, other credits and the completed return.

What if I have little tax payable?

If your credits already reduce tax payable to zero, an additional non-refundable tuition credit generally will not create a cash refund by itself. Eligible unused amounts may be carried forward to a future year when you have tax payable. Keep your T2202 and check the unused tuition balance shown on your latest notice of assessment.

Can tuition be transferred?

In some circumstances, a limited amount of current-year eligible tuition can be transferred to an eligible parent, grandparent, spouse or common-law partner. Limits apply; unused amounts carried forward from previous years generally cannot be transferred later. Consider whether using the credit yourself in a future year may be more beneficial.

Keep your records

Keep your T2202 certificate, review your notice of assessment and make sure the tuition amount claimed is eligible. The tuition paid, the amount available to claim and the actual tax reduction are three different figures.

The takeaway

Tuition credits are not a tuition reimbursement program. They can reduce tax owing and, when tax has already been deducted at source, can increase a refund. The amount of tuition fees paid is not the amount of the refund.
