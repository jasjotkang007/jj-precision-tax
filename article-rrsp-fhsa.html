RRSP & FHSA: What to Know Before You Contribute

Registered savings plans can help with retirement or a future home purchase, but their tax treatment is different. The right choice depends on eligibility, available contribution room, income, goals and when you may need the money.

Important: Confirm your personal contribution room through your CRA account and Notice of Assessment. The information below is general guidance and is not a personalized recommendation.

RRSP: retirement savings

An RRSP is designed primarily to help you save for retirement. Eligible contributions may be deducted from taxable income, up to your available deduction limit. You can generally report a contribution and claim the deduction in a later year, where permitted; contributing and claiming the deduction are separate decisions.

  • Check your RRSP deduction limit and any unused contributions.
  • Employer pension adjustments can affect the room you earn.
  • Withdrawals are generally taxable, except where specific rules or programs apply.
  • The Home Buyers’ Plan and Lifelong Learning Plan have eligibility and repayment requirements.
  • RRSP contributions for the prior tax year may include contributions made during the first 60 days of the next year. Check the CRA deadline for the relevant year.

FHSA: saving for a qualifying first home

An FHSA is available to eligible individuals saving to buy a qualifying first home. Eligible contributions may be deducted, and qualifying withdrawals can be tax-free. The general annual participation limit is $8,000 and the lifetime limit is $40,000, subject to carry-forward and other rules.

  • Your participation room starts in the year you open your first FHSA.
  • Unused room can carry forward, but only within the specific FHSA rules.
  • Contributions and transfers from an RRSP to an FHSA both use participation room.
  • An RRSP-to-FHSA transfer generally does not create a new FHSA deduction.
  • FHSA contributions generally must be made by December 31 to count for that calendar tax year; the RRSP first-60-days rule does not apply.
  • There are rules about qualifying withdrawals, the maximum participation period and what happens if you do not purchase a qualifying home.

RRSP vs. FHSA vs. TFSA

An FHSA may be a strong option for an eligible first-time home buyer. An RRSP may fit retirement planning and can provide a deduction when claimed. A TFSA generally does not provide a deduction for contributions, but eligible withdrawals are tax-free and do not add to taxable income. Compare the account rules, employer matching, time horizon, liquidity needs and your current and expected future tax rates.

Key deadlines to remember

  • RRSP: The deadline for a prior-year contribution is typically within the first 60 days of the next year; confirm the official date each year.
  • FHSA: Contributions count in the calendar year made, so plan ahead before December 31.
  • Tax return: File the applicable tax return and report registered-plan activity as required, even if you are not claiming a deduction for every contribution.
